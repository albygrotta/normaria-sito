Your HESI A2 score: why there is no passing mark

November 9, 2026 · 3 min read

The HESI A2 reports a percentage for each section and leaves the threshold to the nursing program. Which sections count, how schools combine them, and what that means for how a candidate should study.

Candidates often ask what score passes the HESI A2. The question has no answer, because the exam has no passing score. Elsevier, which produces the test, reports a percentage for each section and leaves the threshold to the nursing program. Two schools can look at the same result and reach opposite conclusions.

How the exam is put together

The HESI A2 is a set of independent sections, not one paper. The usual list is reading comprehension, vocabulary and general knowledge, grammar, mathematics, biology, chemistry, anatomy and physiology, and in some programs physics.

Each academic section runs to about 50 questions for the English and mathematics sections and about 25 for the sciences, and each carries a handful of unscored pilot questions mixed in with the rest. Those pilot items are there so Elsevier can test new questions on real candidates. They look exactly like the others and they do not count, which is a reason not to let one strange question unsettle the ones that follow.

Two further parts, the learning style inventory and the personality profile, are not scored at all. They produce a description, not a number.

Why no single number exists

Each program decides two things on its own: which sections a candidate must take, and what counts as good enough.

A nursing school might require four sections; another might require seven. One sets a single threshold on the average, another sets a floor under every section, and a third does both. The common shapes are these:

  • A composite minimum. The required sections are averaged and compared to one figure, often around 75 per cent.
  • A minimum for each section. Every required section must clear the bar on its own, so a strong mathematics score cannot carry a weak anatomy score.
  • Both at once. The strictest version, and the one that catches candidates out.

Most programs sit somewhere between 75 and 80 per cent. The figure is set by the school, appears in its admissions page or its application handbook, and changes from one cycle to the next.

The practical consequence is simple: the target score is whatever the program being applied to says it is. A score that is comfortable at one school can be below the line at the school across the city.

What this changes about preparation

Once the threshold is known, the preparation stops being general.

A candidate applying to a program that averages four sections can trade a weaker subject against a stronger one, and the best use of study time is the section furthest below the average. A candidate applying where every section must clear its own floor cannot trade anything, and the best use of study time is the weakest section, whatever the others look like.

The order of the work follows from that. Find the program's requirements first, write down which sections count and what the thresholds are, and only then decide what to study.

Retakes

Retake rules belong to the program as well. Some allow two attempts in an application cycle, some require a waiting period of 60 days between attempts, and a few allow only one attempt at all.

Where a waiting period applies, it has to be counted backwards from the application deadline. A candidate who sits the exam a month before the deadline and needs 60 days before a second attempt has no second attempt, whatever the rules say on paper.
