Rule 401 Test for Relevant Evidence
Text
Evidence is relevant if: (a) it has any tendency to make a fact more or less probable than it would be without the evidence; and (b) the fact is of consequence in determining the action.
Why it matters
The bar is low on purpose: evidence only has to move the needle a little. What keeps weak evidence out is Rule 403, not this one.
Connections
Rule 402 on admissibility · Rule 403 on unfair prejudice · Rule 404 on character evidence.
Key terms
Any tendency · More or less probable · Of consequence.

The method

Every rule is reduced to four fixed fields: the text, why it matters, the connections, the key terms. The order never changes, and neither does the number of fields.

The reason is practical. After the first few cards a reader knows where on the page the needed fact sits, and attention moves to the content. The structure stops being an obstacle and becomes an index.

  • Text. The rule as enacted, not paraphrased. Exam questions are built on the drafter's words, and an elegant paraphrase drops the decisive one.
  • Why it matters. The reason behind the rule, in two lines. It makes the text easier to hold, and it answers questions put in an unfamiliar way.
  • Connections. The neighbouring rules. The hardest questions live on the border between one rule and the next.
  • Key terms. The three or four words the examiner expects to see. On review they pull back the rest of the card.

Alongside it the books use the Feynman method: a concept counts as learned only when it can be explained in plain words to someone who does not know it. The block «Why it matters» is that explanation, already written.
